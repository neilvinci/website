Case 01Opportunity identification & qualification

The product question that was really a commercial signal.

Not every customer conversation is a sales opportunity. Some are.

US$5,000

Projected ARR generally required for Account Executive involvement. Existing sales-assisted accounts are handled separately.

60+ new-business and expansion escalations supported, within 100+ commercially relevant escalations. Not additional.

  1. Customer conversation
  2. Understand the business need
  3. Spot the commercial signal
  4. Assess value + account context
  5. Facilitate AE engagement
Context
A question about a feature, a plan, a contract, or procurement can point to a bigger requirement underneath: more seats, another team, an expansion, a new-business need.
My role
Recognize the signal, work out what the customer is actually trying to achieve, and assess whether it warrants Account Executive involvement. I facilitate the engagement. The opportunity itself sits with Sales.
Approach
Look past the immediate question to the business requirement. Weigh the need, the projected value, and whether Sales is already engaged on the account. Then hand it over with context, or add what I've learned to an opportunity already in motion.
Business relevance

Commercial needs often surface first in everyday support conversations. Recognizing them early, and routing them properly, connects what customers need with what the business can offer.

Case 02Account engagement & retention

The renewal conversation that explains the account.

Retention signals show up in conversations before they show up in numbers.

Context
Self-service accounts renew monthly. For the 56 India-based accounts I own, part of a pooled portfolio of 300+ and generally organizations with 1–30 seats, that means a recurring conversation about whether the product is working for them.
My role
I own monthly renewal engagement for those 56 accounts: talk with customers about renewing or not renewing, identify adoption challenges and retention risks, and coordinate potential retention interventions.
Approach
Listen for the reason behind the decision. Separate an adoption problem, which consultative guidance can address, from a commercial one, where a discount may be worth coordinating. Bring in the relevant teams when it is.
The decisionRenew, or not renewHeard directly from the customer.
What's behind itAdoption challenge or retention riskIdentified, not assumed.
→
Potential retention intervention, coordinated
Process
  1. Monthly engagement
  2. Understand the decision
  3. Identify adoption and retention risk
  4. Coordinate a potential intervention
  5. Confirm next steps with the customer
Business relevance

Understanding why accounts renew, or don't, gives the business something it can act on: where adoption support helps, where a commercial conversation is warranted, and where neither will change the outcome.

Case 03Cross-functional commercial execution

The account matter that needed more than one team.

Complex account matters rarely sit with one team.

Context
A pricing question, a contract term, a procurement step, a billing issue, or a security review can each pull in different teams, with their own policies and timelines.
My role
I handle complex account-management escalations: connecting the right stakeholders, keeping the customer-facing thread coherent, and following through. Technical escalations stay with the technical teams.
Approach
Establish what kind of matter it is, then who needs to be in it. Hand each team the context it needs, clarify next steps, and keep the customer informed so they aren't left navigating disconnected internal processes.
Process
  1. Understand the matter
  2. Identify the stakeholders
  3. Coordinate internal actions
  4. Keep the customer informed
  5. Follow through
Business relevance

Commercial relationships are won and lost in handoffs. One coherent thread across Sales, Billing, Finance, Deal Desk, and Legal protects the customer relationship while the business follows its own policy and process.

05AI & workflowAn operational capability

Repetitive work shouldn't be manual.

AI and automation are tools for the job, not the job. I use them to take repetitive operational work off frontline teams, so more of their time goes to customers and clients, and to the conversations that need a person in them.

WhereThe problemWhat addresses it

Ticket triage

Every ticket lands in one queue and has to be read before it can be sorted.

Triage agents that sort by type, specialized for billing, account management, and product.

Assignment & routing

The right person finds the case late, or without the context they need.

Agentic configurations that assign and route to the right owner.

Knowledge access

Answers sit in scattered docs and in people's heads.

SOPs and knowledge bases written for the frontline: Guru at Sutherland, Sales and live-chat SOPs at ClickUp.

Repetitive workflows

The same manual steps repeat on every case.

Automations built around the repetitive parts of the work.

Frontline efficiency

Frontline teams spend their day on process instead of people.

Fewer manual steps, so time goes back to customers and clients.

I contribute to these configurations alongside the teams that own them. They support the commercial work. They aren't a separate specialty.

Betterjudgment.

Automation handles the repeatable parts. Judgment is what's left: reading the account, knowing who to bring in, and deciding what actually matters.

That's the part of the job I want more time for.
